# Audience Interview Guide

*Phase 1.1 deliverable. Interviewer: Claude (Opus). Subject: David. Expected 45–90 min, splittable across sessions.*

**Purpose:** produce the raw material for `branding/audiences.md` — 3–5 named audience archetypes, each with a current alternative, a core pain, a hero capability, and a killer objection. Also resolve Open Question A (which capability anchors the headline).

**Method note:** this is a founder-knowledge extraction interview, not user research. David is the source, but he is not the audience — a standing risk is that the audiences described are the ones he finds interesting rather than the ones who will actually adopt. The interviewer's job is to push toward specificity and evidence, not to collect answers.

## Interviewer conduct

- **Demand referents.** Every archetype must be traceable to a real person or a real group David has encountered. "Community organizers" is not an answer; "Taryn, who runs the block club" is the beginning of one.
- **Ask when it last happened.** For each described pain, ask when David last saw someone actually experience it. If the answer is hypothetical, mark it as such — hypothetical pains produce copy that doesn't land.
- **Push back on adjacency.** If an audience is described mainly by how much they'd *like* the protocol philosophically, that's a signal they're an admirer, not an adopter. Separate the two explicitly.
- **Don't accept "all of them."** Force ranking wherever a list appears.
- **Follow the digressions.** The most useful material usually arrives in the aside, not the answer.

---

## Part 1 — Warm-up and framing (5 min)

1. Say the protocol's purpose out loud in two sentences, as if to someone you just met at a party who doesn't work in tech. *(Capture verbatim — this is often better than anything written later.)*
2. What made you start building this? What was the moment or the frustration?
3. Who did you have in mind then? Has that changed?

---

## Part 2 — Current-state context (10 min)

*Goal: understand what the world does today without this.*

4. Think of a specific community you know well that has the problem this solves. What do they actually use now — group chat, spreadsheet, Facebook group, a paid membership tool, nothing?
5. What breaks for them? Where does the current approach visibly fail?
6. Has anyone ever described that failure to you in their own words? What did they say?
7. Is the pain acute or ambient? Do people complain about it, or have they just accepted it as how things are?

> **Probe:** ambient pain is much harder to market against than acute pain. If everything reported is ambient, that's a significant finding and the messaging strategy has to create recognition before it can offer relief.

---

## Part 3 — Audience enumeration (20 min)

8. List everyone you can imagine adopting this. Don't filter yet.
9. Now: which of these would actually *issue cards* — the ones who take the initiating action? Who merely *holds* a card because someone else issued it? *(These are fundamentally different marketing targets; the site is for issuers.)*
10. Cut the list to five. What did you cut and why?

**For each of the five, work through:**

- a. Name the archetype with a referent. Who specifically?
- b. What are they doing today instead?
- c. What's the one thing they'd get from this that they can't get elsewhere?
- d. What's the first objection out of their mouth?
- e. What would have to be true for them to say yes?
- f. Where do they exist online? What do they read, who do they follow, where do they gather?
- g. How many of these people do you personally know? *(Feeds Phase 5.)*

> **Probe on (d):** press until the objection is specific. "It's too complicated" is a placeholder. Is it setup effort, distrust of anything crypto-adjacent, fear of being the only one using it, doubt that their members would install an app, or something else?

---

## Part 4 — Capability ranking (15 min)

*Goal: resolve Open Question A.*

Present the five near-term capabilities:

1. Create and distribute cards
2. Build applications that verify cards
3. Message cards and receive messages from them
4. Matrix multiparty chats gated by card ownership
5. Run your own press, relay, or wallet service

11. Rank these by how compelling they are **to your primary audience** — not by how technically foundational they are.
12. Which one would you lead with if you had one sentence and no chance to explain further?
13. Which is the hardest to explain, and is it worth the explanation cost?
14. Card issuance is the foundation, but Matrix-gated chat may be the most immediately graspable — "a group chat only your neighbors can get into." Argue against leading with the chat. Then argue for it.
15. Is there a capability *not* on this list that people keep getting excited about when you describe the project?

---

## Part 5 — Objections and skepticism (15 min)

16. Where does skepticism live for each audience — privacy, crypto associations, "another platform to maintain," effort to onboard members, doubt it'll still exist in two years?
17. What's the most damaging accurate criticism someone could make of this project right now?
18. When you've described this to people, what's the reaction that most surprised you? What did people misunderstand?
19. How much does the blockchain dependency actually hurt you with the primary audience? Have you seen it land badly in a real conversation?
20. The protocol depends on network effects — a card is worthless if nothing accepts it. How do you answer "why would I be first?"

---

## Part 6 — The pre-launch problem (10 min)

*Goal: find the honest register for the site.*

21. Nothing works for an outside community yet. When you tell someone that, how do you say it? What's your natural phrasing?
22. What's the most concrete thing you could truthfully show someone today — a demo, a screenshot, a test run, a video? *(Resolves Open Question C.)*
23. When do you actually expect capability #1 to be usable by someone who isn't you? How confident are you in that date? *(Resolves Open Question B — status-label granularity.)*
24. What would you want someone who signs up now to feel in six months — that they got in early, or that they weren't strung along?

---

## Part 7 — Assurances (10 min)

*Goal: seed the assurance register (step 2.1).*

25. What can someone count on this protocol to do, always? Say them as promises.
26. What can it explicitly *not* promise? What are the honest limits?
27. Which of these promises is the one that, if broken, would make the whole thing pointless?

---

## Part 8 — Wrap-up (5 min)

28. What haven't I asked that I should have?
29. Of everything we discussed, what's the thing you most want a stranger to understand?
30. What are you most worried about in the marketing of this?
