## Scene 1 — What's a membership card? (0:00–0:10)

- [X] Approved

**Voiceover:**
Think about a library card. A union card. Being a card-carrying member of any community you believe in. A card says: this person belongs here.

**Visual:** Simple line drawings: a library card, then a union card, then a fan of the five community card faces (Waterfront Salsa Society, LGBT Center Youth Group, Neighborhood Mutual Aid Network, Gospel Choir, Tech Workers for Climate Action). The fan settles into a phone.

**On-screen text:** *A card says: you belong here.*

---

## Scene 2 — Handing out cards (0:10–0:26)

- [X] Approved

**Voiceover:**
A membership card does the same thing online. Desiree runs a salsa society. She designs a card, shares a link, and members add it to their phones with a tap. No accounts. No passwords. No membership list to maintain.

**Visual:** The `step1-handoff` illustration, animated. A link goes out and the Waterfront Salsa Society card lands in several phones. Icons for "accounts," "passwords," and "spreadsheet" pop and disappear.

---

## Scene 3 — Access to community resources and spaces (0:26–0:48)

- [X] Approved

**Voiceover:**
Now anyone in the community can set up an online service that checks for that card, like a rideshare list, a calendar anyone can post to, or a free-stuff board. To get in, members just show their card. And if the group likes to chat on WhatsApp, they can stay on WhatsApp.

**Visual:** Three resource icons (car for the rideshare list, calendar for the shared calendar, clothes for the free-stuff board) appear one by one as they're named. Hero moment: the card is dragged from the phone wallet onto a "Members only" door, which opens with a coral highlight. Final beat: a WhatsApp-style group chat (no logo) carrying on undisturbed.

**On-screen text:** *Anything your community makes can check for a card.*

---

## Scene 4 — Proving affiliation to others (0:48–1:05)

- [X] Approved

**Voiceover:**
Cards also work to build trust. When a member of the dance community has an injury, someone replies and attaches a card proving that they're a licensed nurse. Cards can be updated or revoked, so a card shows someone is in good standing.

**Visual:** The salsa society group chat. A member posts that they hurt their ankle at practice. Someone replies with advice and attaches a card, and a small "✓ Registered Nurse" verification chip appears on the reply. Cut to a card updating (a new line is added to it) and then another card greying out to show revocation.

---

## Scene 5 — Private by design (1:05–1:22)

- [X] Approved

**Voiceover:**
Showing a card proves you are trusted by a community, and nothing else. And the cards you hold aren't connected to each other. Your church choir card can't be linked to your LGBTQ youth group card unless you choose to show them together.

**Visual:** One phone holding the Gospel Choir, LGBT Center Youth Group, and Mutual Aid cards. Each card is shown to a different place. Dotted lines between them try to connect and break apart.

**On-screen text:** *Proves one thing. Reveals nothing else.*

---

## Scene 6 — Run by trusted operators, not a tech company (1:22–1:49)

- [X] Approved

**Voiceover:**
There's no large tech company controlling your cards or spying on you when you use them. Cards are run by a network of trusted operators that anyone can join if they prove themselves. No single operator can see who holds what cards or what they do with them, and cards enable community data to be strongly encrypted by default. The protocol is open source, and nobody owns it.

**Visual:** A single tall corporate tower with lines running to everyone fades out. It is replaced by a web of small, varied nodes: a library, a co-op, a community center, a nonprofit. Each node wears its own card badge. A message travels across several nodes, and each node lights up only a fragment of it.

**On-screen text:** *Run by a network of trusted operators, not a large tech company.*

---
